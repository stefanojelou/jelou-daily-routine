Explored: Acquisition & signup funnel → home_variant / has_template at onboarding_started: does landing context change conversion? -- per-variant onboarding->signup completion (the A/B arms' actual conversion rate, never before measured) + within-experiment leak sizing · lens: by home_variant / has_template (landing context)
🆕 NEW (1)
🆕 Landing context measurably marks signup conversion -- homepage arm home_a beats home_b 37% vs 32% (onboarding->signup, p~.03) and template-arrivals convert ~2x; both arms sit below the untagged direct-entry path (52%)
Directly answering today's assigned angle -- does the landing context (home_variant / has_template) captured at onboarding_started change signup conversion? -- and giving the homepage A/B experiment its first actual per-arm CONVERSION read (the ledger has only ever logged the Jul-3 collapse DROPS by variant, never the completion RATE). On the only clean month the experiment has (June 2026: home_a/home_b launched in June -- all of May was 'undefined' -- and July 3 collapsed the funnel, so June is the single measurable window), onboarding_started->signup_completed (prod, unique, 1-day window) splits three ways: home_a completes 36.9% (314/850), home_b 32.1% (350/1,089), and the UNTAGGED/'undefined' entry path 52.3% (1,015/1,939), against a 43.9% blended baseline (1,674/3,813). Two clean reads. (1) The A/B answer: home_a beats home_b by 4.8pp = a 15% relative lift, and it is statistically real (z=2.21, p~=0.027) -- so landing context DOES move conversion, and the currently-split traffic is leaving the weaker arm live. (2) The uncomfortable one: BOTH experiment arms convert well BELOW the untagged path (52%). That is most likely a confound, not proof the homepage hurts -- 'undefined' is not a randomized control but whoever was never bucketed into the experiment (plausibly higher-intent direct/SSO/deep-link entry that skips the marketing homepage, consistent with the ~52% matching May's pre-experiment 48% baseline) -- so read it as 'the homepage-experiment cohort converts lower than the direct-entry cohort', which marks WHO lands on the homepage, not that the page is worse. Sizing the leak (today's twist), on June volumes: shipping home_a's rate to all home_b traffic recovers ~52 signups/month (the clean, actionable number -- kill home_b); if you instead (loosely) held the arms to the untagged 52% path the gap is ~351 signups/month, but that upper bound is confounded by the entry-route selection above. The has_template facet points the same way, harder but on tiny N: the ~1% of onboarding_started that arrive WITH a template pre-selected (has_template=true) complete signup at 78.4% (29/37) vs 43.6% (1,645/3,776) without -- ~1.8x -- consistent with the ledger's template-intent-marks-committed-users theme, but N=37 so directional only. Net: the assigned angle resolves YES -- landing context measurably marks conversion (home_a>home_b, significant; template-arrivals ~2x) -- with the actionable move being to retire the losing home_b arm (~52 signups/mo) and the honest caveat that the arms-vs-untagged gap is an entry-route selection effect, not a proven homepage penalty. Marks not drives.
